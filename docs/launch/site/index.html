Low-profit suggestions

Stuck stock

How it works

Each part does what it is good at. Your rules are enforced by plain code.

Step 1 · Shortlist

Find candidates

Six methods (EASE, ItemKNN, ALS, co-purchase, repeat purchase, popularity) shortlist up to 100 products for the basket.

→
Step 2 · Rank

What will they want?

A LightGBM model, trained on past orders, ranks the shortlist and gives each item a calibrated chance of purchase.

→
Step 3 · Optimize

What earns the most?

A small integer program picks the 2 items with the best chance × profit, under a 15% margin floor and stock limits.

One dial

Choose how far to lean from "most likely to sell" toward "most profit".

Overstock push

Give slow stock extra weight, from 1.0 (off) to 2.0.

Safe fallback

If anything fails, the cart shows your best sellers that pass the margin floor.

Runs on one CPU

No GPU to serve. Built with free, open-source tools.

What did not work, and why we tell you

The original plan put a large language model at the centre. We tested it with a pass/fail rule set before the run.

LLM as ranker: no

Qwen2.5 (1.5B) found the item in its top 5 for 16.8% of baskets, vs 27.6% for LightGBM on the same 1,000 baskets. The 3B model was no better.

LLM as an extra signal: no

Combined with LightGBM, the model gave the LLM almost zero weight. Hit@5 changed by −0.1 points.

What is next for it

A fine-tuned version is being tested against the same rule. If it loses, the LLM is used only for brand-new products and short explanations.
